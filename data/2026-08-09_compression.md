# Certificate compression on the captured corpus, 2026-08-09

Question: certificate compression (RFC 8879) is the mitigation every migration
plan names for post-quantum chain bloat. On the chains real sites actually
serve, what does it recover, and what does that recovery become after migration?

Run against the 2026-08-07/08 capture, no network. Generated by
`src/compress_chains.py`; the interpretation lives in the article.

**Prior art, first, because most of this is not ours.**
`draft-ietf-tls-cert-abridge-02` already measured compression over ~75,000
Tranco chains and published percentiles (Table 1, original 2308/4032/5609,
ZStandard at maximum 1619/3243/3821). It also already states the qualitative
post-quantum conclusion, as does `draft-ietf-uta-pqc-app-03` (lines 864-870).
Measuring compression on real chains is NOT a new contribution and must never
be presented as one. What is ours: the decomposition, the per-algorithm winner,
and the join to this repo's per-site ML-DSA projection.

## Notes on the numbers

- **8,152 compressed, 8,151 projected.** One chain carries a certificate whose
  signature or SPKI did not parse. Projecting part of a chain is worse than
  dropping it, so the whole chain is dropped from the projection only.
- **Best-of-three against cert-abridge's zstd.** The headline 985 B is the best
  of zlib 9, brotli 11, and zstd 22. The draft's row is zstd alone, so the
  comparable figure is the 942 B zstd median. Cite the right one.
- **This corpus compresses better than the draft's.** 2,719 / 3,754 is 72.4% of
  original against the draft's 3,243 / 4,032, or 80.4%. Corpus size, corpus
  composition (top 10k against ~75k), and two years of chain growth are all
  confounded here. Record it as an observation. It is not a trend claim.
- **The saving tracks size, not certificate count** (r = +0.80 against raw bytes,
  r = +0.32 against certificate count). On classical chains that is not a
  surprise: size largely IS structure, since bigger certificates carry more
  SANs, more SCTs, and more URLs. That correlation is exactly what breaks under
  migration, because ML-DSA adds size that is not structure.
- **The projection's load-bearing assumption** is that migration adds no
  compressible bytes. Measured in `pqc-cert-matrix` phase3: an ML-DSA signature
  or SPKI comes out of every RFC 8879 algorithm 4 bytes larger than it went in,
  at every parameter set. That is compressor framing on input with no redundancy.
- **No compression was run against a migrated chain.** The corpus contains no
  post-quantum certificates. Every post-migration figure is Proposed.

## Reproduce

```bash
pip install brotli zstandard
gunzip -k data/capture-top10k.jsonl.gz
python3 src/compress_chains.py \
  --capture data/capture-top10k.jsonl \
  --summary data/summary-top10k.jsonl
```

Runtime is roughly 90 seconds; brotli at quality 11 over 8,152 chains is the
cost, and it is deliberate, since the point is the upper bound on the saving.
